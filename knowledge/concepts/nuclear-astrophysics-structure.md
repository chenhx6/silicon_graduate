# Nuclear-structure inputs to astrophysical reaction networks

## Definition

Stellar reaction networks are sensitive to nuclear masses, thresholds, level densities, single-particle and cluster spectroscopic strength, deformation, electromagnetic matrix elements and weak-transition distributions. The relevant input depends on the reaction regime and thermodynamic conditions.

## Evidence Boundary

[[aprahamian-2005-nuclear-astrophysics-structure]] is a review bridge, not a current rate evaluation or an independent high-spin experiment. `B(E2)`, quadrupole moments and global model deformations can inform reaction calculations but do not by themselves prove γ-softness, triaxiality or octupole rigidity.

## Regime Map

- Low level density / isolated resonances: direct capture, Breit–Wigner or R-matrix treatment.
- High level density with overlapping resonances: Hauser–Feshbach, with explicit `D`, `Γ` and Gamow-window conditions.
- Weak stellar rates: thermally populated parent states, Fermi/GT matrix elements, phase space and possible forbidden contributions.
- Far from stability: mass/deformation model spread and missing levels dominate uncertainty.

## Necessary Assumptions

Map each nuclear-structure input to a reaction regime and uncertainty; do not transfer a spectroscopy label directly into a rate without masses, thresholds, level density and strength information.

## Discriminating Observables

Masses/separation energies, resonance energies and widths, level densities, electromagnetic/GT strengths and weak-rate phase space are the relevant discriminators.

## Supporting Evidence

[[aprahamian-2005-nuclear-astrophysics-structure]] provides the review bridge; this concept is contextual and adds no independent high-spin experiment.

## Counter-evidence and Competing Interpretations

Hauser–Feshbach, direct/R-matrix and shell/cluster descriptions can give different rates when level density or strength functions are poorly constrained.

## Our Current Position

Use this as an input-provenance map, not as a shortcut from deformation vocabulary to astrophysical conclusions.

## Relevance to the Wiki

This map prevents silent transfer of a spectroscopic deformation label into an astrophysical rate. It also provides an L3 bridge for testing which companion observables—masses, `B(E2)`, lifetimes, level densities or weak strengths—would actually change a network conclusion.
