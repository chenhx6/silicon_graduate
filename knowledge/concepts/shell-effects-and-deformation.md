# Shell effects and deformation

Shape-dependent single-particle level bunching can create shell-correction minima at spherical, triaxial, octupole, superdeformed or high-spin configurations. The minima are intrinsic model outputs and must be connected to masses, spectra, lifetimes or transition observables before becoming laboratory claims.

## Definition

Shell effects are structure-dependent energy corrections arising from single-particle level bunching; deformation changes the gaps and therefore the preferred intrinsic minima.

## Necessary Assumptions

Specify the mean-field/pairing model, configuration space, symmetry restoration and rotational frequency before comparing a shell minimum with measured structure.

## Discriminating Observables

Mass residuals, band crossings, moments of inertia, transition strengths, quadrupole moments, superdeformed bands and shape-isomer energies can test the model minima.

## Supporting Evidence

[[ragnarsson-nilsson-sheline-1978-shell-structure]] and [[aberg-flocard-nazarewicz-1990-mean-field-shapes]] provide the historical shell/deformation map.

## Counter-evidence and Competing Interpretations

Configuration mixing, zero-point motion, pairing and projection can move or wash out an intrinsic minimum; a calculated minimum is not a direct laboratory shape measurement.

## Our Current Position

Keep shell-correction minima as model-layer hypotheses until connected to independent spectroscopic or mass observables.
