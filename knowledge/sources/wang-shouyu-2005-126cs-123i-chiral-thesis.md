## Paper Question and Scientific Motivation

- Author-explicit motivation: test disputed `126Cs` band-head spins and signature inversion systematics, and determine whether the positive-parity doublet at the edge of the A≈130 chiral island is a chiral pair (PDF pp.43–47, 123–124).
- Author-explicit question for `123I`: extend the sparse high-spin scheme and test the systematics of iodine rotational bands and band termination (PDF pp.148–150).

## Method and Design Logic

- `126Cs` was populated with 65-MeV `116Cd(14N,4n)` at the Niels Bohr Institute NORDBALL array. The thesis reports an `820 μg/cm²` Cd target, `1 mg/cm²` Au backing, up to 19 BGO-suppressed HPGe plus a LEPS detector, about `8×10^8` γ-γ events, and ADO matrices (PDF pp.47–49, 199).
- ADO calibration uses stretched quadrupole values near `1.4` and dipole values near `0.7`; low values are treated as possible M1/E2 mixtures. ADO is combined with coincidence placement, intensity balance, internal conversion and alignment additivity (PDF pp.47–49, 71–78).
- `123I` is a subsidiary channel of the same `116Cd(14N,5n2p)` data set; the thesis uses coincidence, energy sums, intensity balance and ADO (PDF pp.149–154).

## Key Evidence and Reasoning Chain

1. Coincidence relations and intensity balance establish the `126Cs` level scheme and extend several bands (Figs.3.3–3.4, PDF pp.60–61).
2. ADO, internal conversion and parallel cascades revise the low-lying placement and support the tentative band-head assignments `(9)+, (11)+, (5)−, (4)−, (7)−, (7)−, (8)−` for Bands 1–7 (PDF pp.71–78).
3. Alignment additivity and reduced transition ratios map the bands to quasiparticle configurations; the post-crossing `B(M1)/B(E2)` mismatch is explicitly left to shape/configuration changes (PDF pp.80–89).
4. For the positive-parity Bands 1–2, similar pre-crossing alignment, small excitation-energy separation, similar `B(M1)/B(E2)` and odd-even staggering support the author’s chiral interpretation (PDF pp.124–145).
5. The thesis itself tests that interpretation against `134Pr` electromagnetic/alignment/lifetime discrepancies and concludes that the chiral criteria are not universally decisive (PDF pp.140–145).

## Summary

The thesis establishes an expanded `126Cs` scheme, assigns the halo-band head as `(9)+`, adds more than 100 γ transitions, and argues that the positive-parity doublet is probably a chiral pair. It proposes that the `124–130Cs` positive-parity doublets share common physics, while emphasizing that `134Pr` gives a counterexample to a simple universal fingerprint. The `123I` part extends five bands and interprets two formerly separate ΔI=2 sequences as a common `πg7/2[404]7/2+` oblate collective structure; this is a separate source result rather than evidence for `126Cs` chirality.

## Nuclear Structure Information

- `126Cs`, `Z=55, N=71`: Band 1 is the positive-parity `πh11/2⊗νh11/2` halo band; Band 2 is its positive-parity partner candidate. Bands 3–7 are negative-parity or subsidiary structures with assignments listed above; Band 8 is an unlinked strong-coupling candidate.
- `123I`: Band 5 is the `πh11/2[550]1/2−` prolate/terminating band; Bands 3–4 are assigned to `πg7/2[404]7/2+`; Bands 6–7 are retained as a weakly supported `d5/2[413]5/2+` coupled structure.

## Authors' Interpretation

The author argues that Coriolis effects generate normal signature splitting, while proton-neutron residual interaction, recoil terms and quadrupole pairing contribute anomalous splitting; triaxiality affects the amplitude but is not presented as the sole cause of signature inversion (PDF pp.93–108, 193–197). For `126Cs`, the chiral interpretation is author-level and explicitly probabilistic.

## Model Results

- Alignment calculations use Harris references and additivity of neighboring odd-A quasiparticle alignments (PDF pp.70–84).
- A semiclassical geometrical model is used for `B(M1)/B(E2)`; post-crossing deviations are attributed as possibilities to changing `Q0`, effective `K`, deformation and multiquasiparticle mixing (PDF pp.88–89).
- A particle-rotor calculation reproduces the broad `126Cs` partner spectra and `S(I)` amplitude, supporting the interpretation within the tested model (PDF p.144).

## Competing Interpretations and Limitations

- ADO cannot distinguish E2 from ΔI=0 M1; many spins remain parenthesized. The 217.8-keV transition is a direct thesis-vs-earlier-analysis dispute: the thesis assigns E2 and rejects M1(ΔI=0) through the 54.5/303.3-keV cascade consistency (PDF pp.75–77).
- The chiral criteria are not universal. `134Pr` shows different alignment, `B(M1)/B(E2)` and lifetime behavior, and the thesis itself suggests a γ-vibrational alternative (PDF pp.140–145).
- `123I` Band 6–7 configuration is explicitly adopted because no decisive evidence disproved it, not because it was uniquely established (PDF pp.181–182).
