## Competing Interpretations and Limitations

- The polarimeter uses the \(0^\circ\) and \(90^\circ\) scattering arrangements and evaluates the secondary-ray polarization from corrected count rates and an energy-dependent analyzing coefficient \(Q\). The empirical and Monte Carlo estimates were calibrated with the \(^{60}\mathrm{Co}\) cascade, yielding \(\langle Q\rangle=0.258\pm0.015\) for that calibration. Printed pp.668–670, Sec. 2.
- The authors scan \((\delta_1,\delta_2)\) for each candidate spin, first using prior measurements, then adding their \(P\) data. For the 0.79 MeV level, a prior \(3^+\) minimum at \((0.21,-2.63)\) shifts to the final \((0.210,-1.1)\). The table distinguishes prior coordinates, post-addition \(\chi^2\) values and final coordinates. The reported final errors are statistical. Printed pp.670–671, Sec. 3 and Table 1 notes d–f.
- The capture analysis retains only the spin-2 capture contribution because the subthreshold \(2^+\) resonance dominates thermal capture. Its cascade supplies the orientation conditions. The fit reports no Gaussian \(\sigma/I\) parameter. Its relative opening-angle coefficients and circular-polarization \(R\) have distinct meanings from in-beam \(R_{\rm ADO}\) or \(R_{\rm DCO}\).
- The proposed use as a time-reversal test is the authors' projection based on measured admixtures. This source contributes the linear-polarization observations and the combined fit. The directional and circular-polarization evidence remains attributable to its original publications.
