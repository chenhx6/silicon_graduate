## Summary

Yang et al. synthesized and identified two new neutron-deficient isotopes: $^{160}_{76}\text{Os}_{84}$ and its $\alpha$-decay daughter $^{156}_{74}\text{W}_{82}$.
1. $^{160}\text{Os}$ decays by $\alpha$ emission with $E_\alpha = 7080(26)\text{ keV}$ and half-life $T_{1/2} = 201^{+58}_{-37}\ \mu\text{s}$, measured via genetic correlation with the proton decay of $^{156}\text{Ta}$ ($E_p = 1011(5)\text{ keV}$, $T_{1/2} = 106(4)\text{ ms}$) populated by the $\beta^+$ decay of $^{156}\text{W}$. The production cross section was determined to be $\sigma = 5.4^{+1.2}_{-1.0}\text{ nb}$.
2. $^{156}\text{W}$ ($N=82$) is a $\beta^+$ emitter with a deduced half-life of $T_{1/2} = 291^{+86}_{-61}\text{ ms}$.
3. Ground-state to ground-state reduced $\alpha$-decay widths $\delta^2$ extracted for the $N=84$ isotones systematically decrease above $Z=68$ (Er), with a marked drop at $^{160}\text{Os}$. The authors interpret this decrease in $\delta^2$ as direct empirical evidence for the strengthening of the $N=82$ neutron shell closure toward the proton drip line, supported by mean-field and shell-model predictions (WS4+RBF, HFB-32, LSSM).

## Competing Interpretations and Limitations

### Shell Closure Strengthening vs Yrast Trap Absence
Yang et al. explicitly note:
> "In addition, according to the energy level systematics (Fig. 6 in Ref. [23]) for the $N = 84$ isotones, a $\nu(f_{7/2} \otimes h_{9/2})8^+$ yrast trap isomer is expected to exist in $^{160}\text{Os}$. However, no evidence for the isomer was found in the present work. Only the events observed in the correlations with the proton emissions from $^{156}\text{Ta}(2^-)$ were utilized to determine the decay properties of $^{160}\text{Os}$ and $^{156}\text{W}$." (PDF p. 3, col. 1).

**Physical Implications**:
1. **Mean-Field Shell Gap vs Multiplet Ordering**: The strengthening of the $N=82$ spherical shell closure (manifested in small $\alpha$ preformation probabilities $\delta^2$ and large neutron shell gaps) governs the ground-state mass and binding stability. However, whether an excited state becomes an isomer (an "yrast trap") depends sensitively on the fine energy ordering of multi-quasiparticle multiplets (e.g. $8^+$ vs $6^+$ or $7^-$) and transition rates.
2. **Mechanisms for Isomer Absence**:
   - Fine multiplet rearrangement: As $Z$ increases to 76, proton-neutron monopole interactions shift the single-particle energies of the $\nu 0h_{9/2}$ and $\nu 1f_{7/2}$ orbits relative to other states. If the $8^+$ level moves above a lower-spin state with sufficient transition energy, the $E2$ transition rate increases rapidly with transition energy $\Delta E$, shortening the lifetime below the $\sim 1\ \mu\text{s}$ flight time or the $30–700\ \mu\text{s}$ electronic correlation window.
   - Reaction feeding / detection limits: In the $^{106}\text{Cd}(^{58}\text{Ni}, 4n)$ reaction at $335\text{ MeV}$, feeding to high-spin isomeric states competes with fission during compound-nucleus de-excitation, and any branch decaying via undetected channels would fall below the $5.4\text{ nb}$ production sensitivity.
   - Conclusion: Robust shell magicity does **NOT** automatically guarantee the existence or experimental observation of seniority isomers or yrast traps. Shell persistence and isomerism must be analyzed as distinct physical phenomena.
