# α–γ polarization and mixed transitions in \(^{215}\mathrm{Po}\)

## Bibliographic Record

K. Krien, M. J. Canty and P. Herzog, *Nuclear Physics A* 157, 456–470 (1970). DOI `10.1016/0375-9474(70)90226-5`.

## Scope and Reading Depth

Read all 15 publisher pages, including the experimental setup, Figs. 1–11, Tables 1–4, the spin analysis and the note added in proof. PDF p.1 corresponds to print p.456.

## Summary

The polarimeter fits α–γ linear-polarization correlation coefficients \(B_2,B_4\) from triple-coincidence ratios across particle–photon opening angles. The silicon detector selects the α feeding the level of interest and rejects contaminating lines unresolved in the NaI(Tl) sum spectra. Separate runs use \(^{223}\mathrm{Ra}\) and \(^{227}\mathrm{Ac}\) sources for the nominal 271 and 401 keV γ rays respectively. The paper also measures γ–γ angular correlations and L-subshell conversion. Its tables report \(\delta^2\), the E2/M1 intensity ratio, separately from the sign tests. Printed pp.457–462, Figs. 1–4, Tables 1–2.

## Competing Interpretations and Limitations

- The 130–271 keV γ–γ fit uses points between \(60^\circ\) and \(150^\circ\). The paper attributes the large-angle excess at \(165^\circ\) and \(180^\circ\) to contaminating γ scattering. Its \(A_2=-0.060\) has a different uncertainty and a different coincidence geometry from the α–271 keV coefficient with the same center. Printed pp.458–461, Fig. 2.
- The α–γ analysis varies the α partial-wave mixture and uses spin and parity restrictions plus independent conversion data. The paper includes \(L\le6\) and the two lowest allowed even α orbital momenta under the stated parity/hindrance assumptions. The 401 keV ratio cancels the common particle factor in the adopted model. Gaussian in-beam \(\sigma/I\) is inapplicable to this particle-tagged decay geometry.
- Comparing experimental and single-particle \(\delta^2\) with cited lifetimes leads the authors to favor M1 hindrance over a large E2 enhancement. They retain competing deformation and configuration-mixing explanations. Table 4 uses 0.33 for 130 keV as in Ref. 5 and gives the 271 keV upper error as \(+4.9\), while Table 2 gives \(+4.8\) for that prior result. The 401 keV values rounded to \(Q_\gamma=0.97\) and \(\delta^2=32.3\) are consistent through \(Q_\gamma=\delta^2/(1+\delta^2)\). The note in proof mentions a competing conference assignment without presenting its data. Printed pp.468–470.
