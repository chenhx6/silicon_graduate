- PDF pp.1–87 fully read by sections: spherical/deformed shell effects, harmonic/modified oscillator and realistic potentials, Strutinsky shell correction, axial/reflection asymmetry, diffuseness, experimental comparisons, shape isomers, intermediate-mass nuclei, high-spin shell structure and superheavy stability.
- Not covered: cited primary datasets and post-1978 developments.

## Key Results

- The review frames magic numbers and deformation as the same quantal shell phenomenon: level bunching and enhanced binding produce spherical or deformed stability. Standard spherical magic numbers `2,8,20,28,50,82,126` arise from spin–orbit-modified shell structure (PDF pp.3–15).
- Harmonic/modified oscillator and realistic Nilsson potentials generate shape-dependent shell gaps; the shell-correction method combines a smooth liquid-drop energy with microscopic single-particle corrections to map potential-energy surfaces (PDF pp.6–26).
- Axial asymmetry (`γ`), reflection asymmetry (`β3`) and radial diffuseness alter shell gaps and can stabilize triaxial, octupole, superdeformed, shape-isomeric and superheavy configurations (PDF pp.27–41, 62–73).
- Experimental comparisons use masses, separation energies, 2+ energies, deformation, isomerism, fission barriers and high-spin band termination/superdeformation; high-spin shell effects are treated as rotating-frame configuration stability rather than direct shape measurement (PDF pp.42–82).

## Summary

Ragnarsson, Nilsson and Sheline provide the historical shell-correction backbone for understanding magic numbers, deformation, shape coexistence and high-spin stability. It complements Åberg–Flocard–Nazarewicz by emphasizing shell-gap systematics and observables while retaining the same intrinsic-model boundary.

## Competing Interpretations and Limitations

- Harmonic/modified oscillator, Nilsson and shell-correction predictions depend on potential, diffuseness, pairing and smoothing parameters.
- Mass/deformation/2+ systematics support shell effects indirectly; they do not uniquely identify a triaxial, octupole or superdeformed laboratory band.
- The review predates modern self-consistent EDF, projection and high-statistics spectroscopy; numerical examples are historical context, not current constants.
