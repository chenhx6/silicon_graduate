## Summary

Watanabe et al. reported the first spectroscopic identification of excited states and isomers in the $r$-process waiting point nucleus $^{128}_{46}\text{Pd}_{82}$ ($N=82$) and its neighbor $^{126}_{46}\text{Pd}_{80}$ ($N=80$). In $^{128}\text{Pd}$, an $8^+$ seniority isomer was identified at $E_x = 2151.0\text{ keV}$ with half-life $T_{1/2} = 5.8(8)\ \mu\text{s}$, decaying via a cascade of 75.1, 260.1, 504.4, and 1311.4 keV transitions ($8^+ \to 6^+ \to 4^+ \to 2^+ \to 0^+$). The deduced transition probability $B(E2; 8^+ \to 6^+) = 0.22(3)\text{ W.u.}$ ($0.053(7)\ e^2\text{fm}^4$) is strongly hindered relative to $^{130}\text{Cd}$ ($1.3(1)\text{ W.u.}$), in quantitative agreement with the seniority cancellation in the $\pi(g_{9/2})^{-4}$ proton-hole configuration approaching half-shell. The yrast $2^+$ excitation energy jumps from $693.3\text{ keV}$ in $^{126}\text{Pd}$ to $1311.4\text{ keV}$ in $^{128}\text{Pd}$, while $R_{4/2} = E(4^+)/E(2^+)$ drops from 2.14 to 1.38, demonstrating a spherical, semi-magic structure and robust $N=82$ shell gap down to $Z=46$.

## Competing Interpretations and Limitations

Watanabe et al. deduce a robust $N=82$ shell gap from the high $E(2^+)$ and the purity of the seniority scheme. However, Taprogge et al. (PRL 2014) point out that while $N=82$ remains closed at $Z=46$, the disappearance of proton subshell closures at $Z=38$ and $Z=40$ causes a mutual-magicity loss that predicts shell quenching further below $Z=46$ (towards $^{122}\text{Zr}$ and $^{120}\text{Sr}$). Moreover, direct mass measurements of $S_{2n}$ and $S_n$ in this region (e.g. AME2020 / JYFLTRAP) and subsequent large-scale shell-model studies (e.g. Yuan 2016) show that the shell gap can persist at $Z=46$ while still allowing significant reduction in the neutron separation energy near the neutron drip line.
