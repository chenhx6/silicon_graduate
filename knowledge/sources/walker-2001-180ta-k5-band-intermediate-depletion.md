## Summary

The paper provides a nuclear-structure interpretation for the observed photo-depletion of nature's only naturally occurring nuclear isomer, \(^{180\text{m}}\)Ta (\(J^\pi = 9^-\), \(E_x = 75\text{ keV}\), \(t_{1/2} > 1.2 \times 10^{15}\text{ y}\)), to its short-lived ground state (\(1^+\), \(t_{1/2} = 8.15\text{ h}\)). Direct de-excitation is hindered by \(\Delta K = 8\). Walker et al. demonstrate that previously observed photoactivation resonances at 1010(10), 1220(30), and 1430(30) keV match the energies of the \(8^+\) (1076 keV), \(9^+\) (1298 keV), and \(10^+\) (1538 keV) rotational members of a \(K^\pi = 5^+\) band based on the 592-keV state. This mediates the depletion by dividing the \(\Delta K = 8\) step into two consecutive \(\Delta K = 4\) transitions.

## Competing Interpretations and Limitations

Direct \(\Delta K = 8\) decay from the isomer to the ground-state cascade is heavily \(K\)-forbidden in axially symmetric deformed rotors. The intermediate-state interpretation requires substantial Coriolis or residual-interaction \(K\)-mixing at \(\sim 1\text{ MeV}\) excitation energy. While the energy match is consistent within \(\pm 10\text{ keV}\), direct coincident detection of the back-decay \(\gamma\)-ray was not available at publication time.
