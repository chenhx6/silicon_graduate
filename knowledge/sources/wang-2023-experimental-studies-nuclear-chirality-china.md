## Review Question and Scientific Motivation

The review asks what Chinese-led experiments have contributed to the identification and testing of nuclear-chirality candidates, how the evidence varies across A≈130, A≈100 and A≈80, and which measurements remain decisive. Its organizing principle is experimental systematics, with special emphasis on transition probabilities and coexistence with other approximate symmetries (PDF pp.1-2, 10-15).

## Review Design Logic

- Inventory candidate nuclei, production reactions, laboratories and detector arrays in a single historical table (PDF pp.2-3; Table 1).
- Compare partner bands through excitation-energy differences, `S(I)`, kinematic moments of inertia, alignments and `B(M1)/B(E2)` ratios in the three principal mass regions (PDF pp.3-10; Figs.3-8).
- Treat lifetime-derived absolute `B(M1)` and `B(E2)` as a stronger test than branching-derived ratios, using `130Cs`, `106Ag`, `76Br` and `80Br` as examples (PDF pp.10-13; Figs.9-11).
- Discuss reflection-asymmetric and pseudospin correlations as coexisting/interacting degrees of freedom, using the Br isotopes, `74As`, `81Kr` and `131Ba` as examples (PDF pp.13-15; Figs.12-13).
- Close with an experimental agenda: identify new islands/boundaries, measure more lifetimes and static moments, test coupled symmetries and use upgraded arrays (PDF pp.14-15).

## Summary

Wang et al. synthesize Chinese experimental contributions to a 23-nucleus candidate census and compare the usual spectral fingerprints across A≈130, A≈100 and A≈80. The review is most useful as an evidence-routing and counterexample source: it emphasizes that absolute transition probabilities are rare, that `106Ag` lifetime results argue against treating two superficially similar bands as chiral partners, and that DSAM uncertainty estimates should account for correlated nuisance parameters. It also proposes an isotope-level connection between octupole correlations and the evolution of Br doublet splittings, but that connection is a review-level inference built from heterogeneous experiments and a reflection-symmetric TPRM calculation. No new handed-geometry measurement is supplied.

## Secondary Census

Table 1 lists 23 candidate nuclei reported by Chinese research groups: `74As`, `76,78,80,82Br`, `81Kr`, `84Rb`, `106Mo`, `98Tc`, `110,112Ru`, `104,106,107,110Ag`, `109In`, `123,126I`, `126,130Cs`, `131Ba`, `128La` and `138Pm` (PDF pp.2-3; Table 1). The table also records the production reaction, laboratory and detector array. These entries should be used to find the cited original papers, not to create 23 new source-independent nucleus records.

### Table 1 reaction audit

The printed target for both the `82Br` and `81Kr` rows is extracted and visually reads as `82Ge`: `82Ge(α,1p3n)` and `82Ge(α,5n)`. Those channels cannot produce Br (`Z=35`) or Kr (`Z=36`) from Ge (`Z=32`) by charge conservation. Replacing Ge with Se (`Z=34`) makes both channels charge- and mass-consistent, so `82Se` is the likely intended target, but the original Refs.19 and 20 were not checked in this ingest. The review table must therefore be quoted as printed only with an explicit probable-typographical-error warning (PDF p.3; Table 1).

The closely related iThemba programme review [[bark-2024-investigations-nuclear-chirality-ithembalabs]] prints `82Se(α,5n)` and `82Se(α,p3n)` for the same `81Kr/82Br` studies (Bark 2024 PDF p.9, Table 1). This resolves the conservation problem at the review-lineage level, but it is not independent original-experiment verification.

## Lifetime and DSAM Evidence Boundary

The review's strongest methodological message is not that a single lifetime pattern proves chirality, but that partner-resolved absolute strengths can reject an otherwise plausible assignment. It contrasts agreement cases (`130Cs`, `76,80Br`) with `106Ag`, where the later reported absolute strengths differ and the expected `B(M1)` staggering is absent (PDF pp.10-13; Figs.9-11).

For DSAM, the paper sketches the Doppler-shift relation `E(θ,t)=E0[1+v(t)cosθ/c]` and line-shape fitting, then flags an uncertainty-practice problem: varying one parameter at a time and adding deviations in quadrature does not represent correlated nuisance parameters or asymmetric/physical limits. Its Bayesian/MCMC recommendation is a secondary pointer to Sun et al. 2023, not an independently verified method result in this ingest (PDF pp.11-13).

## Coupled-Symmetry Interpretation Boundary

The Br-isotope comparison in Fig.12 combines measured and calculated average doublet splittings with average intrinsic dipole moments. The reflection-symmetric TPRM cannot itself include octupole deformation; the statement that octupole correlations “catalyze” chirality is therefore a review synthesis inferred from the earlier-than-calculated splitting change and E1-related systematics. It is useful as a testable hypothesis, but it is not a direct measurement of a causal octupole effect or stable octupole deformation (PDF pp.13-14; Fig.12).

## Competing Interpretations and Limitations

- The candidate census inherits the terminology and assumptions of its cited papers; recounting a nucleus in this review adds no independent evidence.
- Similar energies, `S(I)`, inertias, alignments and branching ratios can occur for different configurations; `106Ag` is the review's own counterexample.
- Many plotted ratios are not partner-resolved absolute strengths, and lifetime coverage is sparse.
- The A≈80 neutron-number trend is an interpretation of systematics, not a direct observation of principal-axis angular-momentum geometry.
- The octupole-catalysis proposal compares heterogeneous experimental cases with a model that omits reflection asymmetry.
- Strong phrases such as symmetry breaking or first evidence must be mapped back to the evidence level of each original paper.
- Table 1 contains at least one probable target-isotope typographical error affecting two rows.
