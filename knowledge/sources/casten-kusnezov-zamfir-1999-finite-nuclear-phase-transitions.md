## Summary

For even-even systems selected with \(R_{4/2}>2.05\), changing the abscissa from neutron number to \(E(2_1^+)\) and pooling multiple elements produces a compact \(E(4_1^+)\) trajectory in the region analyzed. The paper combines this empirical observation with IBM intrinsic surfaces and earlier literature on \(^{152}\mathrm{Sm}\). The nearby bend at about 120 keV is a regional energy scale, and the article explicitly allows a different value in other shells.

## Competing Interpretations and Limitations

- The article assembles published spectroscopy, masses and \(B(E2)\). The onset of a regional trend and phase coexistence in one nucleus have different evidence. The examples of two shallow \(^{152}\mathrm{Sm}\) minima are model surfaces, and the \(^{152}\mathrm{Sm}\) spectroscopy used to support coexistence is drawn from cited studies.
- The numerical IBM test holds \(\kappa\) fixed and samples a stated collectivity range. The turnover and slope changes summarize finite-system data. A continuous model control axis and a macroscopic thermodynamic singularity cannot be inferred from the plotted discrete levels.
- Casten's textbook reuses and expands this analysis in [[book-casten-nuclear-structure-simple-perspective]] second-edition §7.4. It supplies no second independent sample for the same trend.
