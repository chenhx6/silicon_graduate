## Summary

Taprogge et al. identified a new excited state at $E_x = 1353\text{ keV}$ in $^{131}_{49}\text{In}_{82}$, the one-proton hole nucleus relative to the doubly magic $^{132}_{50}\text{Sn}_{82}$ core. The state was populated in both the $\beta$ decay of $^{131}\text{Cd}$ ($T_{1/2} = 68(3)\text{ ms}$) and following $\beta$-delayed neutron emission from $^{132}\text{Cd}$ ($T_{1/2} = 97(10)\text{ ms}$). It depopulates via a 988-keV $\gamma$ transition feeding the known $I^\pi = (1/2^-)$ $\beta$-emitting isomer at $E_x = 365(8)\text{ keV}$ ($T_{1/2} = 350(50)\text{ ms}$, $\pi p_{1/2}^{-1}$), rather than the $(9/2^+)$ ground state ($\pi g_{9/2}^{-1}$). Systematics and monopole Hamiltonian considerations firmly assign this $1353\text{ keV}$ state as the previously missing $\pi 1p_{3/2}^{-1}$ single-hole state relative to $^{132}\text{Sn}$.

Incorporating this single-particle energy into realistic shell-model calculations ($V_{\text{low}-k}$ from CD-Bonn), the authors discovered that the compression of proton orbitals ($\pi g_{9/2} - \pi p_{1/2}$ gap only $365\text{ keV}$, $\pi p_{1/2} - \pi p_{3/2}$ gap $988\text{ keV}$) leads to a complete disappearance of proton subshell closures at $Z=38$ and $Z=40$ along the $N=82$ isotonic chain (in sharp contrast to $N=50$ where $^{88}\text{Sr}$ and $^{90}\text{Zr}$ exhibit strong subshell closures). Because the "mutual support of magicities" is absent, the authors extrapolate that the $N=82$ neutron shell gap must undergo significant quenching between Sn ($Z=50$) and Zr ($Z=40$) ($\sim 2.5\text{ MeV}$ reduction), arguing that $r$-process calculations should favor mass models predicting $N=82$ quenching (such as HFB-24) over models with persistent spherical gaps (such as FRDM).

## Competing Interpretations and Limitations

### Hole Reference and Single-Particle State Identification
The hole state is strictly defined relative to the doubly closed $^{132}\text{Sn}$ core ($Z=50, N=82$). In $^{131}_{49}\text{In}_{82}$, the ground state is $(9/2^+)$ ($\pi g_{9/2}^{-1}$ hole), the first excited state is the $365\text{ keV}$ $(1/2^-)$ isomer ($\pi p_{1/2}^{-1}$ hole), and the newly observed state at $1353\text{ keV}$ is the $\pi p_{3/2}^{-1}$ hole. The decay occurs exclusively between the two negative-parity hole states ($p_{3/2}^{-1} \to p_{1/2}^{-1}$, $\Delta J = 1$, $M1/E2$), with no branch observed to the $9/2^+$ ground state due to angular momentum selection rules ($\Delta J = 3$).

### Astrophysical Extrapolation vs Empirical Stability at Z=46
Taprogge et al.'s argument for $N=82$ gap quenching relies on the concept of "mutual support of magicities" and is an extrapolation to experimentally unreached isotones at $Z=38$ (Sr) and $Z=40$ (Zr). In contrast, Watanabe et al. (PRL 2013) directly measured $^{128}_{46}\text{Pd}_{82}$ ($Z=46$), demonstrating a high $E(2^+)=1311\text{ keV}$ and pure seniority hindrance in $B(E2)$, which shows that $N=82$ shell structure remains robust down to $Z=46$. These studies investigate different proton numbers ($Z=46$ vs $Z=38, 40$) and represent distinct empirical vs extrapolated claims rather than a direct contradiction at the same $Z$.
