# Unexpected rapid variations in odd-even level staggering in γ-vibrational bands

## Scope and Reading Depth

- Full text, abstract, Fig.1 and conclusions read; nuclei `104–106Mo`, `108–112Ru`, `112–116Pd`, `156–170Er` compared.
- Not covered: raw cubes, B(E2) values and modern microscopic calculations.

## Key Results

- γ-band odd-even level staggering changes rapidly with neutron number and spin; `104Mo`, `108Ru`, `112Pd` and `112Ru` display different/reversed patterns.
- `112Ru` develops particularly large high-spin staggering (up to ~570 keV), while Sm–Pt nuclei generally show smaller/no reversal except `170Er`.
- Simple Davydov–Filippov vs Wilets–Jean classifications cannot explain all reversals; the authors call for microscopic γ-soft/stable-triaxial descriptions and reject backbending as a complete explanation.

## Summary

Gore *et al.* provide a compact warning that γ-band staggering is dynamic, spin- and isotope-dependent, and not a one-number γ-rigidity classifier.

## Competing Interpretations and Limitations

The article is short and reports energy systematics without raw transition strengths or a unique model fit; staggering can reflect γ softness, stable triaxiality, configuration changes or backbending. The comparison is not an independent lifetime/electromagnetic-strength test.
