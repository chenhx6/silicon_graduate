# `194Tl`

## Identity

`Z=81, N=113` odd-odd nucleus with previously reported candidate chiral structures. Guo 2022 tests their configuration region with constrained RMF and predicts additional possibilities.

## Early INGA Crossing Evidence

Pai 2012 independently studied `194Tl` with `185,187Re(13C,xn)` at 75 MeV using INGA. The observed B1 band is built on a `293.1-keV`, `8−` level; its γ-ray energies and relative intensities extend the scheme to `18−`. The alignment plot derives an approximately `5ℏ` gain near `ℏω≈0.34 MeV` after subtracting a stated Harris reference. These are level/transition observations and an energy-derived alignment, respectively; the authors' explanation as a second `νi13/2` neutron-pair alignment, changing B1 from 2qp to post-crossing 4qp, is a configuration interpretation (not a directly measured label). See [[pai-2012-high-spin-bands-194tl]] PAI12-1/2/3/4.

The B2 `18ℏ` bandhead near `3.16 MeV` is not well developed. Its 761-keV link has DCO consistent with quadrupole character but no IPDCO; the assumed E2 character gives only tentative negative parity, while M2 remains possible. Pai explicitly identifies the `18−` lifetime as necessary to resolve this ambiguity. A post-crossing configuration is a systematics-based proposal, not a firm assignment (PAI12-5/6).

## Model Structure

C*/H* have `(β,γ)=(0.14,36.81°)` and `(0.17,18.80°)` with `πh9/2⊗νi13/2^-1`. I* at `(0.13,45.19°)` is a higher four-quasiparticle `πh9/2⊗ν(i13/2)^-3` candidate above the crossing.

## Evidence Boundary

Pai 2012 provides a direct INGA dataset for the earlier B1 crossing (above). It is distinct from the later iThemba/AFRODITE data lineage. The crossing itself was therefore already reported before the later post-crossing four-quasiparticle near-degeneracy claim. The latter remains secondary in this Wiki: Bark 2024 reports the Masiteng 2013/2014 results, but those full texts were not accessible for locator-level reanalysis, so its 4qp partner grouping and DSAM claims are not upgraded to primary-source evidence here (BK24-12/13).

Guo 2022 supplies constrained minima, not partner spectra or transition strengths; its higher four-quasiparticle candidate remains predictive.

[[bark-2024-investigations-nuclear-chirality-ithembalabs]] summarizes a two-quasiparticle pair continued through a crossing into `πh9/2⊗νi13/2^-3` four-quasiparticle bands. The post-crossing pair stays within `110 keV` and reaches `37 keV` at `I=21`; however, DSAM-derived absolute strengths are similar for all three observed negative-parity bands. A four-band rotor calculation leaves pair grouping non-unique and predicts one unobserved band (BK24-12/13; PDF pp.16-20, Figs.20-27).

## Production Reactions

The iThemba review lists `181Ta(18O,5n)` at `91-93 MeV`, including a separate backed-target DSAM run. These are secondary run metadata; the original articles remain required (BK24-7; PDF p.9, Table 1).

The earlier Pai 2012 dataset used `185,187Re(13C,xn)` at 75 MeV with INGA; it is an independent acquisition from the later iThemba `181Ta(18O,5n)` / AFRODITE studies, while sharing the physical B1 band lineage ([[pai-2012-high-spin-bands-194tl]] PAI12-1; [[bark-2024-investigations-nuclear-chirality-ithembalabs]] BK24-7/12).

## Bands

No new band page is created. The known pair is secondary evidence, while C*/H*/I* are constrained minima.

## Competing Interpretations

Two-quasiparticle chirality, a post-crossing four-quasiparticle system and triaxial shape coexistence require separate band/configuration mapping. Similar absolute strengths do not uniquely select which three of four same-configuration model bands form partners.

## Open Questions

- Does the I* four-quasiparticle minimum generate an observable partner pair?
- Are absolute strengths compatible across the reported and predicted systems?
