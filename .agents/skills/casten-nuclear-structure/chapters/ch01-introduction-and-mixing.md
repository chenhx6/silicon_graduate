# Chapter 1: Introduction, Nuclear Force, and Mixing

## Core Idea
Casten builds a physical vocabulary before formal machinery: saturation and the Pauli principle shape the nuclear landscape, while two-state mixing explains avoided crossings and many apparently complicated trends.

## Key Concepts
- **Two-state Hamiltonian**: diagonalize two unperturbed states coupled by `V`.
- **Level repulsion**: observed splitting is `sqrt(Delta E_u^2+4V^2)`.
- **Strong mixing**: equal admixtures occur near degeneracy.
- **Transition cancellation**: a large mixture can have a small interband E2 when intrinsic moments nearly match.

## Worked Example
For `R=Delta E_u/V`, the smaller component tends to `1/sqrt(2)` at `R=0` and to `1/R` for `R>>1`. The same two-state algebra is the first diagnostic for configuration mixing, Coriolis mixing, and band crossings.

## Boundary
The two-state picture is a control model. Multistate and continuum coupling require explicit extension.
