# Chapter 2: The Nuclear Landscape

## Core Idea
Masses, binding energies, separation energies, level schemes, reaction selectivity, `E(2+_1)`, `R_4/2` and `B(E2)` organize where spherical, vibrational and deformed structures occur.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Landscape-to-structure mapping**: use separation-energy trends and magic-number gaps to identify shell evolution before assigning collective models.
- **Systematics before labels**: compare neighboring isotopes/isotones rather than classifying one level scheme in isolation.

## Key Concepts
Valley of stability; magic numbers; mirror nuclei; binding energy; separation energy; shell closure; deformation onset; beta decay; collectivity.

## Worked Example
Casten compares the near-closed-shell and midshell limits. Near magic nuclei, `E(2+_1)` is high, `R_4/2 < 2`, and `B(E2)` is small. In the rotational region, `R_4/2` approaches 3.33 and `B(E2)` is large. The A≈130 region is explicitly shown as a transition/systematics region, so a single level ratio should be read together with transition strengths.

## Boundary
`R_4/2` and `B(E2)` are empirical structural indicators. They do not separately identify fixed triaxiality, gamma softness, wobbling or chirality.
