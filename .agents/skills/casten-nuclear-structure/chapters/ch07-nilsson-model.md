# Chapter 7: The Deformed Shell Model or Nilsson Model

## Core Idea
Deformation splits spherical single-particle orbitals into Nilsson levels whose slopes, asymptotic quantum numbers and prolate/oblate behavior provide an intuitive map of intrinsic configurations. The Nilsson wave function describes intrinsic orbital motion; rotational coupling must be added separately.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Nilsson-diagram reading**: infer orbital motion from slope, symmetry and Fermi-surface location before diagonalizing a full Hamiltonian.
- **Asymptotic labeling**: use `[N n_z Lambda Omega]` to connect orbit identity with deformation-driving character.
- **Pseudo-crossing analysis**: near-degenerate orbitals with the same `K` repel and exchange spherical parentage; do not read a post-crossing line as a fixed configuration.
- **Unique-parity purity**: opposite parity and energy isolation suppress mixing, making high-`Omega` unique-parity orbitals useful configuration benchmarks.

## Key Concepts
Nilsson Hamiltonian; asymptotic quantum numbers; `K/Omega`; `N`; `n_z`; `Lambda`; unique-parity orbit; prolate; oblate; deformation-driving orbital; pseudo-crossing.

## Worked Example
Casten uses a prolate deformation to relate orbital-plane inclination to `K/j`: equatorial, low-`K` orbitals generally slope down, while polar, high-`K` orbitals slope up. In the `N=82-126` region, the positive-parity intruder from the `i_13/2` shell is isolated among negative-parity orbitals and remains especially pure. At intermediate deformation, nearby same-`K` orbitals mix and repel; at large deformation the `[N n_z Lambda] Omega` labels become the useful asymptotic description.

## Boundary
The orbit sequence is model- and deformation-dependent. Pairing, residual interactions, triaxiality, angular-momentum projection and continuum effects can change near-Fermi configurations and must be included for quantitative spectroscopy.
