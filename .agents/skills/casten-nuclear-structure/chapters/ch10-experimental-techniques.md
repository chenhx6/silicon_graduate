# Chapter 10: Selected Experimental Techniques

## Core Idea
Choose a reaction and detector strategy by the matrix element or structural constraint it can actually determine; population alone is not a complete structural measurement.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Coulomb-excitation control**: stay below nuclear contact, control distance of closest approach and scattering angle, and extract E2 matrix elements through a coupled multistep analysis.
- **Back-angle substate simplification**: near 160-175 degrees, approximate m=0 excitation reduces the number of unknown substates; 55 degrees is useful because P2 vanishes approximately for common E2 angular distributions.
- **Spectroscopic completeness**: ARC and related techniques use known capture spin/parity and primary-transition selection rules to constrain whether levels are missing within a specified excitation window.
- **Reaction-to-question matching**: fusion-evaporation populates high-spin cascades; heavy-ion transfer is direct and spin/configuration selective; Coulomb excitation targets electromagnetic collectivity.

## Key Concepts
Distance of closest approach; semiclassical parameter eta; adiabaticity parameter xi; multistep Coulomb excitation; virtual excitation; ARC; spectroscopic completeness; fusion-evaporation; transfer selectivity.

## Worked Example
For Coulomb excitation, progressively stronger projectiles or more backward scattering reduce the closest approach and open higher-step excitation. The analysis must retain virtual paths, feeding and relative signs of matrix elements. Measuring projectile-gamma coincidences can separate direct population from decay feeding.

## Boundary
The chapter is an introductory design map, not a substitute for modern coupled-channel codes, detector-response calibration or a full statistical analysis. Its most reusable contribution is the explicit mapping from observable to structure question.
