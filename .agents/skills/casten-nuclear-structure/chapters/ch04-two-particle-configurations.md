# Chapter 4: Two-Particle Configurations and Residual Interactions

## Core Idea
The residual interaction rearranges two-particle spectra according to angular momentum, isospin, radial overlap and multipole content; the short-range delta interaction and the `J=0` pairing force provide complementary control models.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Delta-function residual interaction**: use as a transparent short-range benchmark for matrix-element systematics.
- **Multipole decomposition**: separate monopole, pairing, quadrupole and higher multipoles before interpreting level shifts.
- **Geometry of coupled particles**: relate two-particle angular momentum to spatial overlap and interaction range.

## Key Concepts
Residual interaction; delta interaction; pairing; multipole expansion; monopole shift; particle-particle; particle-hole; isospin; pairing-plus-quadrupole.

## Worked Example
For two identical nucleons in an equivalent orbit, the attractive delta interaction lowers `J=0+` most strongly. Casten explains the result geometrically: the Pauli principle suppresses contact for aligned high-`J` motion but permits more average contact for the anti-aligned `J=0` configuration. The lowering grows with the number of magnetic substates, approximately with `2j+1`, while the remaining levels compress toward the unperturbed position.

Pairing retains only the `J=0+` channel but allows pair scattering between different orbits. The longer-range quadrupole term is therefore needed in addition to pairing when deformation and collective motion are being modeled.

## Connects To
This chapter supplies the microscopic bridge from shell structure to collectivity and to the pairing/Coriolis competition in high-spin bands.

## Boundary
The delta interaction is a short-range model and its radial scale is not an experimental observable. `T=0` proton-neutron interactions and multi-`j` configuration mixing can change the ordering; use the two-particle result as a diagnostic baseline.
