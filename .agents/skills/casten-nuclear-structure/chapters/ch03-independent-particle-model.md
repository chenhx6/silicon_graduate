# Chapter 3: The Independent Particle Model

## Core Idea
A mean field plus `l^2` and spin-orbit terms produces shell structure and single-particle orbit ordering; residual interactions then explain deviations from the independent-particle limit.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Mean-field baseline**: establish central-potential and spin-orbit shell ordering before adding correlations.
- **Shell-closure diagnostic**: large gaps and separation-energy changes identify robust closures, subject to monopole evolution.
- **Unique-parity benchmark**: isolated orbitals can provide relatively pure configurations for high-spin tests.

## Key Concepts
Central potential; harmonic oscillator; `l^2` term; spin-orbit splitting; `jj` coupling; shell closure; single-particle energy; valence particle/hole; unique-parity orbit.

## Worked Example
The harmonic oscillator groups levels by `2n+l`, but does not give the empirical magic numbers. A realistic surface-like potential plus a strong spin-orbit term lowers `j=l+1/2` orbitals enough to reproduce the observed closures. The intruder/unique-parity orbital in a major shell is often isolated in energy and parity, making it a useful high-spin configuration benchmark.

## Connects To
Chapter 7 deforms this baseline into Nilsson orbitals; Chapters 4-5 add residual interactions, pairing and seniority. The unique-parity idea connects directly to A≈130 `h_11/2` and to high-spin alignment analyses.

## Boundary
The orbit sequence is a mass-dependent guide. Residual interactions, core excitations and deformation shift effective single-particle energies; a Nilsson or high-spin assignment cannot use the spherical ordering without a configuration audit.
