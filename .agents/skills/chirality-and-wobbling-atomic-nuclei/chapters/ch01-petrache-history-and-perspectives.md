## 1. Executive Summary & 25-Year Roadmap

This chapter chronicles the 25-year evolution of nuclear chirality and wobbling from their theoretical inception by Stefan Frauendorf in 1997 to contemporary experimental campaigns using high-resolution multidetector arrays (Gammasphere, Euroball, GASP, JUROGAM, AGATA, and GRETA).

### Key Historical Milestones:
1. **1997**: S. Frauendorf & J. Meng predict nuclear chirality based on Tilted Axis Cranking (TAC) calculations (Nucl. Phys. A 617, 131);
2. **2001**: K. Starosta et al. discover the first chiral doublet bands in odd-odd $N=75$ isotones ($^{134}\text{Pr}$) using Euroball (Phys. Rev. Lett. 86, 971);
3. **2001**: S. W. Ødegård et al. discover transverse wobbling in triaxial superdeformed (TSD) $^{163}\text{Lu}$ (Phys. Rev. Lett. 86, 5866);
4. **2003**: S. Zhu et al. identify candidate chiral doublets in odd-$A$ nucleus $^{135}\text{Nd}$ (Phys. Rev. Lett. 91, 132501);
5. **2004**: T. Koike, K. Starosta, and I. Hamamoto formulate the electromagnetic selection rules (KSH rules) for chiral partner bands (Phys. Rev. Lett. 93, 172502);
6. **2014**: C. M. Petrache et al. discover five pairs of chiral doublet bands in even-even nucleus $^{136}\text{Nd}$ ($M\chi D$, Phys. Rev. C 97, 041304(R));
7. **2015–2022**: The $^{135}\text{Pr}$ controversy unfolds: J. T. Matta et al. claim transverse wobbling in normal-deformed $^{135}\text{Pr}$ (PRL 2015), followed by N. Sensharma et al. claiming two-phonon wobbling (PRL 2020), until B. F. Lv et al. (PRC 2022) falsify the wobbling interpretation using Clover linear polarimetry ($11.1\sigma$ rejection).

## 2. Four Chiral Mass Islands

The chapter identifies four distinct regions on the Segrè chart where high-$j$ valence particles and holes couple with a triaxial core:
- **$A \a$A \aA \a130$ Island ($Z \approx 55 - 62, N \approx 71 - 79$)**: The most prolific region ($\pi h_{11/2} \otimes \nu h_{11/2}^{-1}$), exhibiting both 2-quasiparticle, 3-quasiparticle, and 4-quasiparticle chiral bands;
- **$A \a100$ Island ($Z \approx 44 - 48, N \approx 57 - 63$)**: $\pi g_{9/2}^{-1} \otimes \nu h_{11/2}$ configurations in Pd, Rh, and Ag isotopes with pronounced $\gamma$-softness;
- **$A \a80$ Island ($Z \approx 35 - 37, N \approx 41 - 45$)**: $\pi g_{9/2} \otimes \nu g_{9/2}$ in Br and Rb isotopes, featuring chiral-octupole parity quartets;
- **$A \a190$ Island ($Z \approx 79 - 81, N \a113 - 117$)**: $\pi h_{9/2} \otimes \nu i_{13/2}^{-1}$ in Tl and Pt isotopes with strong Coriolis decoupling.

## 3. Horizons for Next-Generation Tracking Arrays

The chapter concludes with experimental requirements for resolving remaining controversies using AGATA (Advanced GAmma Tracking Array) and GRETA (Gamma-Ray Energy Tracking Array):
- Pulse Shape Analysis (PSA) and $\gamma$-ray tracking yielding position resolution $< 5\text{ mm}$;
- Unprecedented linear polarization sensitivity ($Q \ge 0.25$ up to $1.5\text{ MeV}$) using Compton scattering events between segmented HPGe interaction points;
- Sub-picosecond DSAM lifetime measurements with zero Doppler broadening contamination.
