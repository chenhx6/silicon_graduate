## Theory/analysis exercise

I classified twelve baseline statements into four layers: (1) experimental fact—an observed γ energy, coincidence, intensity or ADO value; (2) author interpretation—band/configuration labels; (3) model result—PES, NPA or empirical-shell output; and (4) Codex inference—what additional observable is required before a collective-mode claim. A minimal reconstruction used the (^{128})I sequence (8^-,285.2	o9^-,696.2	o10^-,1041.7	o11^-,1454.0	o12^-,1873.5) keV (D12-9; PDF pp.84–85): the energy differences are 411.0, 345.5, 412.3 and 419.5 keV. The arithmetic verifies placement consistency only; it does not prove a rotor, configuration, or triaxial shape.

The reusable rule is: energy closure supports a level placement; ADO supports a geometry-specific multipolarity constraint; neither alone proves (J^pi), configuration, or a collective mode.

## Counter-evidence and missing companion observables

The counterexample is the thesis’s own model/experiment mismatch: NPA reverses parts of the (6^-/7^-) ordering and places candidate (8^-) states around the observed one (D12-8; PDF pp.82–83). Thus agreement of one calculated energy is insufficient. The necessary companions are transition-level mixing ratios and/or linear polarization, lifetimes for (B(E2))/(B(M1)), absolute strengths or (Q_t), and independent linking/transfer constraints. Shared thesis lineage means repeated citations to this document are not independent evidence.
