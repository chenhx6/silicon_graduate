## Theory/analysis exercise

I used a compact occupancy cross-check to make the shell-gap language operational. A spherical major closure requires filling the degeneracy of each `j` orbital, `2j+1`; for the HJS spin–orbit ordering the high-`j` intruder can create a closure after a lower oscillator group. For example, the sequence through `N=50` is consistent with cumulative capacities `2+6+10+2+4+8+6+12=50` when the spin–orbit split partners are counted in the historical ordering. The arithmetic checks a closure count; it does **not** estimate an energy gap.

For a deformed high-spin case, the analogous calculation is a rotating-frame level-density comparison: identify the occupied Nilsson branches, track their crossings with rotational frequency, and compare the local spacing before and after the crossing. The required inputs are a specified potential/deformation parameterization, pairing prescription and frequency grid. Since no numerical level table or covariance was supplied in the source pages, I stopped at the design level and did not invent a gap in MeV. The result is therefore a **model-design exercise**, while the closure count is a transparent arithmetic check.

## Counter-evidence and missing companion observables

Magicity is not established by one high excitation energy or one model plot. At least two necessary companion observables are: (1) masses or one-/two-nucleon separation-energy trends across neighboring isotopes/isotones, and (2) collectivity indicators such as `E(2+)`, lifetime-derived `B(E2)` or quadrupole moments. For a high-spin deformation claim, add band crossings/alignment and transition-strength systematics; for an orbital assignment, add independent spin/parity or transfer/decay information.

Counter-evidence includes pairing changes that mimic separation-energy curvature, configuration mixing that shifts a nominal single-particle level, γ-soft potential-energy surfaces that blur a fixed deformation label, and model-parameter changes in diffuseness, pairing or smoothing that move a calculated gap. The 1978 review explicitly places these dependencies in its model boundary (pp.6–41 and 42–82). Thus a single `E(2+)`, a single mass kink, or a single Nilsson diagram is insufficient.
