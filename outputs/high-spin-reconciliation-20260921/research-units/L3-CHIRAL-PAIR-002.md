# L3: minimum evidence for candidate chiral partners

## Question

When do near-degenerate bands warrant a chiral-partner interpretation rather than crossing, different configurations or shape coexistence?

## Evidence and competing hypotheses

- `134Pr/136Pm`: Petrache 2006 shows multiple crossings and a `134Pr` `Q0,1/Q0,2=2.0(4)` estimate under a two-band mixing model (PDF pp.1–4, Figs.1–4, Table I).
- `136Nd`: Mukhopadhyay 2008 finds markedly different partner `B(E2)` in one early negative-parity pair and favors distinct configurations with band mixing (PDF pp.2–6, Table II, Figs.3–5).
- `135Nd`: Mukhopadhyay 2007 reports more nearly equal partner transition rates and TAC+RPA chiral-vibration/static-chirality interpretation (PDF pp.2–4, Figs.2–4).
- Later `136Nd`: Petrache 2018 strengthens D5 with partner-resolved ratios, while D1–D4 remain weaker candidates; explicit 3D aplanar calculation is shown only for D3 (PDF pp.3–6, Figs.3–6).

## Decision

Near-degeneracy and band links are candidate-level evidence. The minimum useful independent test includes verified band identity and multipolarity, alignment/crossing map, and partner-resolved absolute strengths or lifetimes over the same spin interval. TAC/PRM geometry adds model support but does not replace these observables. Each pair gets its own confidence state.

## Reverse test

If two bands have unequal `B(E2)`/`B(M1)` after common stopping/feeding treatment, or if a configuration-sensitive observable separates them, downgrade the chiral-partner hypothesis even if their energies remain close. A positive result requires the competing crossing/configuration model to fail on the same observables.

## L4 readiness and stop

The literature comparison is complete at L3. No common raw lifetime/branching/covariance package is supplied for a cross-experiment L4 fit. Reopen for a named pair when partner-resolved measurements or public analysis artifacts exist.
