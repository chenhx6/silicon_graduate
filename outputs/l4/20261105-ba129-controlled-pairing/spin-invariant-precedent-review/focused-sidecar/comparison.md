**Nomura2016 Fig.10 is the strongest precedent for the proposed physical interpretation.** PRC94,064310, author PDF p.9 Eq.(8), p.10 discussion and p.11 Fig.10(a–d) define the average of five downward B(E2) values into a bandhead, for DeltaJ=1 or 2. For negative-parity Sm bands, the two choices give different trends; the text explicitly attributes this to weak/strong coupling systematics. It already defeats any broad claim that restricted odd-A indicators were assumed to measure only core deformation.

What the cited analysis does not supply is the present same-state, fixed-J comparison with complete q2/q3, retained-channel saturation, signed spin-pair cancellation and a diagonal-versus-inelastic partition. The proposed study must explain what that decomposition teaches beyond the known channel dependence. Its bandhead sum is downward and averaged by five, so numerical comparisons must include detailed balance and normalization.

**Rouoof2025 Sec.IV C is the strongest precedent for the computational comparison.** PRC111,054309, author PDF pp.12–13, uses a network of 187 E2 elements per nucleus, within/between four bands through I=10, and compares it with the subset having experimental counterparts. Page16, conclusion item2, reports smaller dispersions when the experimental restriction is imposed on the model network. The same paper links configuration mixing with differences between microscopic invariants and energy-staggering classifications.

Thus “restricted versus larger network” and “microscopic mixing changes a shape diagnosis” are already demonstrated. The narrower distinction here is **saturation within one selected angular channel versus complete closure in the declared space**, with an odd-particle/core-spin mechanism. R25's larger network is not complete physical Hilbert-space closure. Its experimental masking test is also a useful method to adopt, with credit, when comparing the Ba calculation with a sparse measured network.

**Lawrie–Orce2025 is the strongest contrary precedent to dismissing “effective” few-element observables.** The publisher original is ADND164,101730, June2025 issue, available online **April 25, 2025**. Pages2–3, Eqs.(5)–(10), distinguish E2-tensor triaxiality gamma from inertia asymmetry Gamma using four elements. Their two-element reductions use the empirical Gamma(gamma) relation, Eq.(11), and the ratios in Eqs.(12)–(15). They compare with Kumar–Cline and generalized-rotor estimates, Fig.3, for selected even-even rotational 2+ states, with mixing-related exceptions.

Therefore a reduced scalar or ratio can be physically useful under a stated regime. Agreement need not mean complete closure, and disagreement is not automatically an algebraic error. Their rotational-state requirement and empirical Gamma(gamma) relation are substantive applicability conditions. They do not provide a general two-matrix-element shape inversion for a mixed odd-A 11/2 state. Applying their gamma curves directly to Ba11/2 would require a new justification.

**What would constitute the distinct result?** The strongest remaining argument has three connected parts:

1. **A specific mechanism:** identify which core-spin components and interactions generate the redistribution, using Huygens' controlled comparisons. Show how that same change alters signed loop sectors and the conversion domain; do not infer causation from one reduced R=0 probability.
2. **A quantitative decomposition:** distinguish total moments, channel strength, diagonal/inelastic same-spin contributions, and cancellation. The calibrated 135Ba artifact has positive spin-pair contributions \(+0.290856\) and negative contributions \(−0.318974\ e^3b^3\), leaving \(q_3=−0.028119\ e^3b^3\). This cancellation is real within the model, but its aggregate factor alone is not diagnostic: 129Ba has an even larger factor while its proxy remains in-domain.
3. **A falsifiable matrix-element consequence:** test a specified static/crossover or same-spin/crossover ratio, with the same input Hamiltonian and operator, rather than treating the angle coordinate as the directly measured result. The [observable note](observables.md) gives candidates and the data they actually require.

None of these claims is established merely by the absence of a matching paper. These are differences from the inspected source passages, not a priority certificate.

**Strongest remaining scientific gap, even if the causal controls succeed:** validation of the **odd-state diagonal and relevant transitional E2 matrix elements**. Calibration to each even core's first 0-to-2 strength fixes a normalization datum; it does not validate the odd-state static moment or the off-diagonal network responsible for cancellation. The parent specifically flags the model \(Q_s(137{\rm Ba})=+0.772963\ e\,b\) for Galileo's benchmark. This sidecar has not judged agreement with an experimental value.

That gap is especially consequential because about 90.0% of 135Ba's calculated same-spin q2 strength is the static diagonal term. A successful algebraic/coupling control could explain an internally consistent but physically inaccurate matrix-element pattern. Conversely, passing appropriate static and transitional checks would make the channel-decomposition result substantially more than a numerical demonstration. Where a datum was used to set eF or another parameter, describe its reuse as a consistency check, not an independent holdout.

The immediate decision should therefore follow the calibrated matrix-element benchmarks and causal result, not expanded broad searching or journal positioning. If those benchmarks fail materially, retain the result as a defined model/regime study and narrow any statement about actual odd-Ba charge structure. If they pass and the controls identify the mechanism, the contribution becomes a specific explanation with an independently testable observable consequence. In neither case is a correction to the source authors' figures presently supported.
